Check an X‑ray for a broken bone

Upload an X-ray of a hand, arm, leg, hip or shoulder. A model trained on X-rays from the public FracAtlas dataset estimates whether it shows a fracture, shows where it looked, and marks where the break most likely is.

A student research project. It is not a diagnosis; a doctor must read every X-ray.

Reading the result

The fracture score

How strongly the classifier thinks the X-ray shows a fracture, from 0 to 100%. Any score above the flag line (9.7%) is flagged. The line is set low on purpose, so the model catches about 9 in 10 fractures; the price is more false alarms.

Where the model looked

The coloured glow is a Grad-CAM heatmap: the parts of the image that pushed the score up. It is coarse, built on a 12 by 12 grid, so it shows the area the model used rather than the fracture line itself. In testing its brightest point sat right on the fracture in about 1 in 5 X-rays.

Likely fracture location

A second model, trained on boxes that radiologists drew around fractures, marks where the break most likely is. Boxes are only shown when the X-ray is flagged. In testing its top box landed on the fracture in 75 of every 100 fractured X-rays. A solid box scored at least 0.73; a dashed box is the best guess with lower confidence.

Where it is less reliable

  • Plates, screws and other implants. The models often treat metal as a sign of fracture, and the box tends to land on the implant: only 4 of 11 such fractures were boxed correctly.
  • Children's X-rays. Growth plates near the ends of long bones can look like a fracture line and cause false alarms.
  • Anything that isn't a plain X-ray of a limb or hip. CT or MRI scans, spine and skull images, and photos of a screen were not part of training.

              About the model

              Two models work together. A ConvNeXt-Tiny classifier decides whether the X-ray shows a fracture; a Faster R-CNN detector marks where. Both were trained on FracAtlas (4,083 musculoskeletal X-rays, 719 of them fractured), split so that near-identical images and image series never sit on both sides of the training and test sets. The numbers below come from the held-out test set, scored once.

              Built by Akinchan Jain, B.Tech CSE (AI), Bennett University. Code, methods and every result on GitHub.

              Fracture Check is a research demonstration and not a medical device. Do not use it to decide whether to seek care. Images are processed in memory to produce the result and are not stored.
